Ethical funding policy
DFF fundraises under the guidance of its Ethical Funding Policy, which outlines the type of funding DFF accepts and the types of activities donations and other funding are used for. DFF’s Ethical Funding Policy seeks to ensure that DFF can properly manage its priorities and projects with appropriate independence from its funders.

Grant Programme

The Digital Freedom Fund financially supports the digital rights community to advance human rights in digital spaces through strategic litigation. We raise funds from foundations and other human rights supporters, and re-grant to the most strategic legal action and resea

--- relevant lines ---
Since 2018, we have supported more than 100 groups and individuals, funded through 164 grants worth EUR 5.5 million.
See information about our grantee partners and their work on our case studies page , where you can filter by different criteria, including grant type, issue area, country and organisation. See answers to our frequently asked questions here .
How to apply for a grant
We consider grant applications through regular open calls.
The most recent call for applications closed in February 2026, with grants approved in June 2026. We expect the next call for applications to run from late November 2026 to mid-February 2027.
Make sure you scroll down to read more information to prepare your application, including the scope of our grants and the criteria by which your application will be assessed. Click here to see more detail about how to submit your application. On the same page you can also find the times for upcoming ask us anything calls.
To help write your application in advance, check out the application guides here , see examples of the digital rights work we are supporting on our case studies page , and read this blog to see a summary of our last round of grant approvals.
Types of grants​
Currently we accept grant applications for two types of activities litigation track support and pre-litigation research support. Since 2024, litigation track support grants can include post-litigation activities. See more about post-litigation support here .
Litigation track support grants can also include post-litigation activities, such as advocacy or enforcement activities following the judgement made in the final instance of litigation.
To be considered for funding, cases need to go beyond the individual by advancing digital rights through legislative, policy or social change and increasing capacity and collaboration in the digital rights community.

The application process consists of the following steps:
Registration and eligibility check
The first step in applying for DFF support is to register and complete an eligibility check. DFF offers two kinds of grant support: litigation track and pre-litigation research. Register and submit your eligibility check on our online grant platform using the link at the bottom of this page.
An eligibility check can be submitted at any time while a call for applications is open, but we recommend submitting your eligibility check as early in the call period as possible so you have more time to work on your application (we recommend at least 1 month if not longer before the deadline). DFF aims to respond to eligibility checks within one week, but usually takes 1-2 working days.
DFF carries out the eligibility check. This is not an assessment of project quality, only of high level mandate criteria, like whether the project focuses on digital rights litigation and fits DFF s geographic scope.
Please submit your eligibility check in our online grant platform (link at bottom of page) before the given deadline in order to be considered. It will not be possible to start a new application request after the deadline.
