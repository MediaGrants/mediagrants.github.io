Access grants that support projects reducing social and economic disparities in Europe. Funding is awarded through open calls managed in each country, open to public bodies, NGOs, businesses and research institutions.

Partnerships between organisations in donor and beneficiary countries are central to the Grants. They foster knowledge sharing, joint solutions and stronger bilateral relations across Europe.
The Grants are funded by Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway and support 15 EU Member States. Funding levels reflect each country’s needs, ensuring balanced growth and inclusion.
The Grants focus on key priorities such as the green transition, innovation, education, culture, good governance, public health, justice and civil society. Each programme is tailored to national needs and European priorities.

General introduction

--- relevant lines ---
Through the EEA and Norway Grants, cooperation is encouraged between entities in Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway and the 15 Beneficiary States. As bilateral project partners, you take part in hands-on cooperation built on shared values and mutual learning.
Part 3 - Information about further cooperation opportunities under the funds for bilateral relations for both Donor and Beneficiary state entities.
Part 4 - A glossary with information about some of the main stakeholders under the Grants.
Disclaimer: The information given here is intended to provide general guidance on frequently asked questions that come up in the context of bilateral partnership under the EEA and Norway Grants. This space is not part of the legal framework of the EEA and Norway Grants and cannot replace consulting the applicable rules on your partnership. Neither the Financial Mechanism Committee (FMC), Norwegian Ministry of Foreign Affairs (NMFA), the Financial Mechanism Office (FMO) nor any person acting on their behalf can be held responsible for the use made of the guidance given here.
The EEA and Norway Grants offer entities from Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway to work in partnership with entities across 15 Beneficiary States to address the common challenges across Europe. As a Donor project partner, you can gain access to European professional networks, international experience and cultural competence through direct involvement in joint bilateral projects. Working with project partners in the Beneficiary States can also strengthen your organisation's visibility, expertise and reach.
Please note that specific eligibility criteria might apply to the individual calls for proposals. These criteria are outlined in the relevant call texts and may further specify the types of Donor project partner entities that are eligible.
The EEA and Norway Grants support cooperation across a range of programme areas , each designed to address concrete challenges in Europe.
You can also become a Donor partner in activities funded under the Civil Society Fund (CSF) in a Beneficiary State. See further information here .

International Partner Organisations

International Partner Organisations and ins

--- relevant lines ---
The Fund for Capacity Building and Cooperation with International Partner Organisations and Institutions contributes to strengthened capacity and cooperation in the Beneficiary States and across the region to promote democracy, the rule of law, human rights, social inclusion, resilience and the European green transition.
Read more about the fund
Through extensive, long-standing co-operation, the Council of Europe and the EEA and Norway Grants support member States in implementing European standards.
The Council of Europe is a key partner for the EEA and Norway Grants, thanks to its various bodies and instruments, which cover a wide range of important areas. This co-operation focuses on Council of Europe’s unique role in translating international standards into practical outcomes to ensure impactful and sustainable interventions.
It also enables precise identification of gaps and challenges across member States. These insights inform the development of a high-level strategy for the Grants, guaranteeing they are implemented in ways that improve the quality of life of people and democratic governance in the beneficiary countries.
European Union Agency for Fundamental Rights
The Agency cooperates with the EEA and Norway Grants as a strategic partner in the protection, promotion and fulfillment of fundamental rights. The cooperation aims to support the design and implementation of EEA and Norway Grants funded programmes and projects in selected EU Member States. It also aims to raise awareness on fundamental rights, combating social exclusion and discrimination, and supporting related civil society initiatives.

How to apply

Do you want to apply for EEA and Norway Grants funding but don‘t know what steps to take? The followi

--- relevant lines ---
ng section answers some key questions about the Grants and how to apply.
We fund projects in 15 countries in Europe. This means you need to be based in one of them in order to apply. The countries are:
What is funded
We fund a large variety of topics, ranging from climate change and energy to cultural cooperation and civil society support. However, we do not cover all of them in every country. Instead, we prioritise the funding to what is most relevant in each country. This means that in order to be eligible for funding, your project idea needs to fit into one of the programmes in your country and contribute to its overall objective.
When & how to apply
Whenever funding becomes available, we announce it through official calls. Each call includes information on what kind of activities are funded, who can apply, the application deadline and all application instructions. It’s important to remember that all project applications need to be submitted through these calls – we do not accept spontaneous applications via email or phone .
If you are from Iceland, Liechtenstein or Norway, you cannot apply directly for funding but you can participate as a partner. More information about patnerships under the Grants will follow soon.

Polish cities can apply for over PLN 700 million from the Norway Grants

Polish cities can apply for over PLN 700 million from the

--- relevant lines ---
 Norway Grants
06/10/2026
From the project "Jarocin-the City of Tomorrow" under the Local Development Program in Poland 2014-2021.
Three calls under Poland's Local Development Programme opened on 23 October. They will fund projects that make Polish cities more sustainable, smarter and better prepared for crises.
Local governments in 100 Polish cities can now plan their bids for more than PLN 700 million (EUR 168 million) from the Norway Grants. The funding comes through the Local Development Programme, the largest programme of the 2021–2028 EEA and Norway Grants in Poland.
Jan Szyszko, Secretary of State at Poland's Ministry of Funds and Regional Policy, announced the calls on 5 October at the Congress of Urban and Regional Policy in Kraków. The Ministry runs the programme and will accept applications from 23 October 2026 to 22 January 2027.
Jan Szyszko, Secretary of State at Poland's Ministry of Funds and Regional Policy.
Photo: Ministry of Development Funds and Regional Policy
"Local development today is not only about infrastructure and public services, but also about a city's ability to respond to new challenges in cooperation with residents and partners," said Aleksandra Wacko, Counsellor for the Norway Grants at the Royal Norwegian Embassy in Warsaw.
Resilient City has another PLN 199.6 million for the same 32 cities. It funds projects that help cities withstand natural, technological and military threats. Examples include early warning systems, protecting critical infrastructure, upgrading shelters and countering disinformation.
See the full list of eligible cities for each call
Full funding and Norwegian partners
Projects will be 100% funded, so cities do not have to contribute any money of their own. All projects must be finished by 30 April 2031.
The Local Development Programme continues a programme of the same name from the previous funding period, which supported 29 medium-sized Polish cities that were losing social and economic functions.
Poland is the largest beneficiary of the EEA and Norway Grants 2021–2028, with EUR 925 million. The Local Development Programme is funded only by Norway, through the Norwegian Financial Mechanism.
The eligible cities, the call details and the application rules are on the EEA and Norway Grants Poland website.
