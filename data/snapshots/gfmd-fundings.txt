Status: Open
Type: Fellowship

Theme: Media support

Funding Size: €3,800
Deadline: 15/11/2026

Eligibility: Journalists between 18 and 45 years of age working as freelancers, trainees, or editors at German media outlets (German applicants), or journalists from Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway, Sweden, Estonia, Latvia, or Lithuania (Nordic and Baltic applicants)
Target region: Germany or Baltic countries
Application language: English (Language skills in German are not a prerequisite)
The Deutsch-Nordeuropäisches Journalistenstipendium, established in 2000, promotes professional exchange and dialogue among journalists from Germany, the five Nordic states, and the three Baltic states. Up to 22 journalists are selected annually: 

--- relevant lines ---
approximately half from Germany travel to a Nordic or Baltic media outlet for an eight-week placement, while the other half from the Nordic and Baltic countries travel to a German outlet. The programme includes a multi-day introductory conference in a Nordic capital or Berlin, the eight-week placement, and a closing event in Berlin at which fellows present their journalistic work and a written experience report.
Eligibility

The €3,800 payment provides partial support only; applicants must be prepared to make a personal financial contribution toward travel and accommodation.

The application deadline is 15 November 2026. Applications should include:
letter of recommendation by an editor, supporting the application
The digital application is kindly requested in one PDF document to: theo@ijp.org

Organisation: World Conference of Science Journalists (WCSJ)

Status: Open
Type: Fellowship

Theme: Media support

Funding Size: Travel-costs covered
Deadline: 23/10/2026

Eligibility: Journalists and journalism students unable to attend the WCSJ2027 without financial aid
Target Region: Global (must be able to procure a visa for the UK)
Application Language: English (AI translations are allowed)
The WCSJ2027 Travel Fellowships support journalists (journalism may be their primary or secondary profession), journalism students, or students in related fields, who would otherwise be unable to attend the World Conference of Science Journalists 2027 (WCSJ2027) in London on 6–9 September 2027. These fellowships aim to broaden international participation, increase diversity, and improve access for professionals 

--- relevant lines ---
who have limited funding opportunities in their own countries or organisations.
Successful applicants will receive financial support towards travel, visa costs, limited local transport and up to six nights of accommodation, together with full conference registration, refreshments and lunches during the conference. The fellowship does not include per diem payments.
Eligibility
Applicants must be eligible for a visa to the United Kingdom
Media professionals who cover science and related fields, such as engineering, climate or health, as part or all of their work can apply. They may be staff members or freelancers, may work part-time or full-time for the media sector, and may work in print, online, broadcast, podcasts or other media formats.
Students in science journalism, journalism, media studies, science communication or related fields may also apply. Students must be of legal age in their home country and able to travel independently.
Those carrying out research and teaching in journalism, media studies or science communication may also apply. They must have a proven track record in the media as authors or producers. Examples of work will be requested.
How to apply
Applications must be submitted through the online application form. Apply here .

Organisation: International Women s Media Foundation

Status: Open
Type: Grant

Theme: Freedom of expression

Funding Size: USD 2,500–USD 10,000
Deadline: Ongoing

Eligibility: USA journalists writing about discrimination
Target Region: USA
Application Language: English
The IWMF s Fault Lines Reporting Fund supports journalists reporting on the forces driving racism, antisemitism, Islamophobia, misogyny, and other forms of intolerance promoting hate in the United States. Successful applications examine how false, divisive, and harmful narratives — such as stories that promote or normalise hate, discrimination, and the dehumanisation of individuals and communities — take hold, including what is fuelling them, where they are most pronounced, the scope of their impact, and where individuals and communities

--- relevant lines ---
 are making real progress in combating these hate-based agendas. The three-year programme provides grants to staff and freelance journalists planning well-researched, investigative, and evidence-based reporting within the United States. Grant funds may be used to cover travel, logistics, security, insurance, and other reporting-related expenses.
Eligibility
The applicant s primary profession must be journalism; applications will be accepted from freelance, staff, or content-based journalists.
A completed application in English (reporting itself may be conducted in any language).
A well-developed story pitch demonstrating investigative merit, originality, reach, potential impact, and need for funding.
Grantees must publish in a U.S. outlet within one year of receiving a grant and must credit the IWMF.
How to Apply
Submit the application through the IWMF Submittable portal on a rolling basis; there is no fixed closing date for the current programme cycle. Larger awards are considered on a case-by-case basis. Apply here .
