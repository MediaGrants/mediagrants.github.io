During her fellowship, Birgit Poopuu will focus on writing her ERC grant proposal, which is guided by the question: How should we think about violence today? Poopuu argues that this question has acquired renewed urgency as contemporary political projects increasingly consolidate "death-worlds"—worlds in which the political rendering of certain people as killable, alongside forms of slow destruction, have become normalized features of world politics. Rather than treating violence as an event or crisis, the project begins from the premise that our recurring reliance on the language of "crisis" reflects a deeper conceptual failure in how violence is understood within international relations and beyond. What forms of knowledge have been excluded from dominant accounts of violence? Whose experiences remain marginal to the production of knowledge about violence? And which connections between different forms of violence are routinely severed in mainstream analyses? By foregrounding these questions, the project develops an alternative analytical framework for understanding violence—one that confronts the death-worlds that contemporary political projects continue to cement.

Facing the Future

Anna Wojciuk's current research focuses on the future of liberal democracy in the context of deep political polarization, democratic backsliding, and the growing challenge posed by contemporary radical rightwing movements: what happens when the traditional center right—historically one of the key stabilizing pillars of liberal-democratic orders—weakens, radicalizes, or loses its capacity to sustain co

--- relevant lines ---
During her fellowship, Wojciuk intends to comparatively and theoretically research the question of how democratic systems can remain stable and legitimate in societies characterized by durable ideological disagreement and increasingly incompatible visions of what constitutes good political order. Taking as its departure point the arguments laid out in the book Let's Agree on Poland: A Case Study in Strategic Constitutional Design (Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2026), which she co-edited with Maciej Kisilowski, the project explores whether the current crisis of liberal democracy should be understood not merely as democratic decline, but also as a broader transformation of democratic governance, legitimacy, representation, and constitutionalism amid cultural fragmentation and geopolitical uncertainty. In particular, it examines how different democratic systems respond to long-term polarization, how constitutional orders adapt to persistent ideological conflict, and whether new forms of institutional design can help democratic societies preserve both stability and political pluralism.

ERC Mentoring Initiative

FaultierJA

CONTACT

In case of any questions, please do not hesitate to contact us at [email protected] .

The Institute for Human Sciences (IWM Vienna), together with the Polish Academy of Sciences and generously supported by the Polish Ministry of Science and Higher Education, has established a mentoring initiative for applicants for European Research Council (ERC) Starting and Consolidator Grants in the humanities and social sciences from Central, Eastern, and South-Eastern Europe . This mentoring initiative is addressed in particular to researchers in

--- relevant lines ---
 the areas of history, anthropology, political science, sociology, science and technology studies, as well as cultural and literary studies, aiming to submit in 2028 – 2029.
Internationally established scholars, who have served as members of ERC evaluation panels, or who have held ERC grants themselves, will act as mentors during a workshop. The aim is to give a small group of mentees individualized advice regarding the development of their projects' academic content through intensive discussion with each other and with the mentors. The initiative's workshops focus on the conception and design of the academic research projects and are meant to complement the work of the National Contact Points (NCPs) for the ERC program, who are able to provide detailed information on the ERC's grant schemes and the application process.
The next workshop will be held remotely on 4–5 December 2026 . Participation is free; the workshop language is English. If you are interested in taking part, please send an email with your application to the email address below. The application should consist of three parts:
an abstract according to the specifications required for the cover of a B1 application (max. 2,000 characters);
a two-page short version of the extended synopsis according to the specifications required for the section a of a B1 proposal;
a curriculum vitae according to the recommended template of the section b of a B1 application.
Applicants are requested to follow the specified formatting guidelines and hand in their application in one Word document .
Please note that all participants will be required to present their project proposals to the mentors and other participants.
As places are limited, please send your applications as soon as possible, but no later than 16 October 2026 . A further workshop is planned for next year.
