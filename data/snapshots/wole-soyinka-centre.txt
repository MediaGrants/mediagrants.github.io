Migrants’ Perilous Sahara Journey in the Spotlight at Exhibition by Soyombo, Former WSCIJ Awardee


--- relevant lines ---
October 28, 2026
The travails of victims of human trafficking was put in the spotlight at Fisayo Soyombo’s Dessert from the Desert, an exhibition of photographs attended by Motunrayo Alaka, Executive Director, Wole Soyinka Centre for Investigative Journalism (WSCIJ), Motunrayo Alaka, on Monday, 26 October 2026, at Alliance Française, Ikoyi, Lagos. The exhibition by Soyombo, founder of the Foundation for Investigative Journalism (FIJ), documents the dangers faced by migrants travelling through the Sahara Desert in search of better opportunities abroad.
Speaking at the exhibition, Soyombo said the project was intended to help prospective migrants make informed decisions by presenting the realities of the perilous journey, rather than the accounts promoted by human traffickers. He explained that travellers risk dying when vehicles break down and water supplies run out, stressing that the extreme conditions make the desert a very challenging place for human survival.
Soyombo’s work has a longstanding connection with WSCIJ. He won the online category of the Wole Soyinka Award for Investigative Reporting in 2014 , was n amed Nigerian Investigative Journalist of the Year in 2016 and won the award again in 2017 . Alaka s attendance reflected WSCIJ’s continued support for investigative journalism and visual storytelling as tools for public engagement. The Centre’s Visual Realities programme trains and mentors photojournalists and cartoonists in visual storytelling, while the Wole Soyinka Award for Investigative Reporting recognises excellence across photojournalism, print, broadcast, online and editorial cartooning.
L-R: PR & Marketing Comms Expert Obianuju Lilian Ikegbune, founder Foundation for Investigative Journalism, Fisayo Soyombo, Founder/Editor-in-Chief, TheCable News, Simon Kolawole, and Executive Director/CEO, WSCIJ, Motunrayo Alaka. The exhibition illustrates how investigative journalism can extend beyond exposing wrongdoing to document lived experiences and make complex social issues accessible to wider audiences. The exhibition also prompted a show of solidarity with migrants stranded in Libya, as participants pledged financial support towards facilitating their repatriation.

40 Years After Dele Giwa’s Killing, WSCIJ Decries Enduring Impunity, Calls for Justice

October 19

--- relevant lines ---
, 2026
The Wole Soyinka Centre for Investigative Journalism (WSCIJ) expresses deep displeasure over the unresolved killing of renowned Nigerian journalist, Dele Giwa, 40 years after he was murdered through a letter bomb on October 19, 1986. Four decades later, the failure to bring those responsible to justice remains a troubling indictment of Nigeria’s commitment to press freedom, accountability and democratic governance.
