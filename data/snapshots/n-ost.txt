The BMZ provides development assistance to a total of 65 countries.This assistance also includes funding for media development.
The mission of the EVZ Foundation is to keep the memory of National Socialist persecution alive and to work for human rights and international understanding. It was founded in 2000 by the German industry and the federal government to make compensation payments to the millions of forced laborers who suffered from the German Nazi regime.
Deutsche Postcode Lotterie is a licensed social charity lottery in Germany where players win prizes together with their neighbors while raising funds for local green and social projects. It actively supports initiatives like the Media Forward Fund to back public-interest journalism and social cohesion.
Visegrad Fund
The International Visegrad Fund supports regional cooperation between civil society organizations to advance their relations, exchange and share ideas and promote mutual understanding. When promoting European values in Central Europe, we work together towards a better-connected future.
The Robert Bosch Stiftung is active in the areas of health, education, and global issues. Through its funding, it works for a just and sustainable future. The Foundation is rooted in the legacy of the entrepreneur and founder Robert Bosch.
JX Fund
The JX Fund supports media and journalists who have fled war and crisis regions, providing them opportunities to quickly and with flexibility continue their work in exile.

Call for Applications: Life Under Occupation 2.0

Published on Sep 8, 2026

n-ost is looking for journalists from Ukraine, Germany, the Czech Republic, Bulgaria and Moldova to take part in Life Under Occupation 2.0, a cross-border journalism programme dedicated to reporting on the realities of Russian occupation. Selected participa

--- relevant lines ---
nts will join online workshops, form reporting teams and receive support for joint publications. Apply by 20 September 2026.
More than four years into Russia's full-scale invasion, and more than a decade since Russia began occupying parts of Ukraine in 2014, the destruction continues. Russian attacks damage homes, power plants, heating systems, transport infrastructure and cultural landmarks. In territories that have lived under Russian occupation for years, the consequences reach deep into everyday life: access to Ukrainian education and independent information remains restricted, families are displaced, property is seized, and people face pressure to accept Russian documents and institutions.
Following the first edition of Life Under Occupation in 2025, n-ost is launching a second edition of the cross-border journalism programme with support from the Friedrich Naumann Foundation. Together with its Ukrainian partner, the Public Interest Journalism Lab (PIJL), n-ost will bring together eight journalists from Ukraine, Germany, the Czech Republic, Bulgaria and Moldova.
The programme will begin in October 2026 with four online workshops. Their provisional themes will broadly cover the nature and everyday realities of occupation, trauma-sensitive reporting, security and safety, and reporting on Crimea .
Who can apply?
How to apply
Please submit your application using this form.
The deadline for applications is 20 September 2026 at 23:59 CEST .
Applicants will be informed of the selection results by 30 September 2026. The online workshops and networking meetings will take place in October 2026. Participants will be expected to complete and publish their projects by the end of March 2027.
If you have any questions about the programme or the application process, please contact Oksana Mamchenkova at mamchenkova@n-ost.org.
