Grants and Investments
We have awarded more than 16,000 grants since our founding in 1888.
What We Fund
We fund individuals working on projects in science, conservation, storytelling, education and technology that align with one or more of our focus areas.
A grant from the National Geographic Society means more than just funding! Learn what it really means to be a National Geographic Explorer and get to know our community.
We offer grant opportunities at various entry points to provide a dynamic pathway to join our community and engage with us.
Whether you are an aspiring Explorer or already a luminary in the field, each opportunity has unique criteria and benefits to ensure that our Explorers receive support and funding aligned to their specific needs and goals. Each year, a small number of grants are awarded to individuals who are just beginning their National Geographic journey, as well as those who are working on more advanced projects.

For Grant Seekers
Our Approach to Grantmaking
Historically, our grants program was structured around an "open call" application model to support general exploration, research, conservation, storytelling and technology initiatives.
To maximize our collective impact, the National Geographic Society has transitioned from an open-call model to a specific Request for Proposals (RFP) style Grants Program.
How the Grants Program Works
Our grantmaking is now driven by distinct, time-bound RFPs designed to address specific environmental, cultural and scientific priorities.
Targeted Scopes: Each funding opportunity features a unique grant posting outlining the specific goals, criteria and outcomes we are looking to support.
Aligned Funding: Rather than applying to generic tiers (like our historical Level I or Level II funding levels), project budgets and funding amounts are tailored to the parameters specified within each active RFP.
Eligibility & Deadlines: Because every RFP is unique, eligibility requirements, geographic focuses and submission deadlines will vary by opportunity.
We encourage all prospective applicants to carefully review our active funding opportunities to find a specific RFP that directly matches their project s scope, expertise and objectives.
How to Apply
All applications must be completed and submitted through the National Geographic Funding Portal. Project applications must be submitted to a specific funding opportunity and should meet the unique scope of the chosen RFP. We do not accept general project applications or submissions shared via email or mail.
Step 1. Browse Open RFPs — Review the list of current funding opportunities on this page to identify an open RFP that aligns with your project s goals and scope. To begin the application process, click the Apply button on the specific RFP grant posting.
Step 2. Create Your Funding Portal Account — If you are a new applicant, you must register and create an account on the National Geographic Society Funding Portal . Returning applicants can log in using their existing credentials.
Step 3. Start the Specific RFP Application — Once logged into the Funding Portal, navigate to your chosen RFP and click the Apply button to begin the online grant funding application.
Step 4. Submit via the Funding Portal — Complete the required project, budget, and collaborator information directly within the online application. Ensure your application is fully submitted through the portal before the stated RFP deadline.
Please note: because the application requirements are specific to the unique scope of each RFP, we strongly recommend thoroughly reading the guidelines for your chosen opportunity before starting your application in the Funding Portal.

The Grosvenor Teacher Fellowship
Apply Now
Meet the 2026 Grosvenor Teacher Fellows .
The Grosvenor Teacher Fellowship (GTF) is a professional learning opportunity for pre-K–12 educators made possible through a collaboration between Lindblad Expeditions and the National Geographic Society.
Through a comprehensive application process, we select educators to host aboard National Geographic-Lindblad Expeditions’ voyages for a life-changing, field-based experience. Grosvenor Teacher Fellows transfer their onboard experience into transformative ways to teach students, engage colleagues, and bring new geographic awareness into their learning environments and communities.
Apply Now
Fellows take on a two-year commitment to support National Geographic’s education and community engagement initiatives.The fellowship was founded by Sven-Olof Lindblad in honor of Gilbert M. Grosvenor, chairman emeritus, National Geographic Society, in recognition of his decades-long work supporting pre-K–12 educators and promoting geography education across the United States and Canada.
During the first year of their commitment, Fellows attend an in-person workshop in Washington, D.C. and embark on a transformative voyage with National Geographic-Lindblad Expeditions to gain new geographic perspectives. Throughout the fellowship, participants engage in virtual gatherings with other Fellows and complete various deliverables, such as student action projects and outreach events, to bring their experiences back to their classrooms and communities.
