How Mongolia s Milee app aims to build media literacy skills
Ole Tangen Jr
August 17, 2026
An app developed by the Faro Foundation, with support from DW Akademie, makes critical thinking and digital literacy training accessible across Mongolia, including in remote rural areas.
Byambajargal Ayushjav saw her fellow Mongolians struggle with digital and media literacy and decided to do something about it Image: Faro Foundation Byambajargal Ayushjav never set out to build a media and information literacy app. The founder of the Mongolian NGO Faro Foundation originally worked i

--- relevant lines ---
In 2015, Ayushjav and two partners launched Hippo Card, a mobile application designed to help Mongolians learn foreign-language vocabulary. The response exceeded expectations. According to Ayushjav, Hippo Card became one of Mongolia s most downloaded apps, reaching 1.2 million downloads in a country of about 3.6 million people.
But the app s rapid growth also exposed other skills Mongolians were in need of. As users sought help with payments, accounts and online transactions, Ayushjav and her team saw firsthand how vulnerable many people were to scams, phishing attempts and the misuse of personal information. Those experiences led to the creation of the Faro Foundation in 2018, focused on digital empowerment and online safety.
The Milee app can be used in Mongolian, Kyrgyz, Uzbek and English (Russian is in development) Image: Faro Foundation That challenge helped inspire Milee. Ayushjav said the team realized that turning educational content into a mobile application could make learning accessible regardless of location, while allowing people to learn in their own time.
"We just launched our product, Milee, for the general public, and we have had very positive feedback from people. So we keep adding more lessons to our application," Ayushjav said.
