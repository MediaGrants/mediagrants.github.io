Facing the Future Fellowship

The Facing the Future fellowship programme supports established and emerging thinkers in reimagining liberal democracy.

Responding to a time when the promises of the liberal order are being questioned, the programme combines independent research, critical exchange and public engagement to inject fresh thinking into concepts such as freedom, solidarity, sovereignty and belonging.

The crisis confronting liberal democracy is not simply a crisis of institutions. It is a crisis of ideas. Across the world, the core promises of the liberal order have lost their lustre and credibility for many of the people they were meant to serve. The rise of illiberal movements is not an anomaly but a symptom of deeper tensions and transformations that politics are scrambling to address.

In the 2026-27 academic year, the Facing the Future Fellows are Gazela Pudar Draško, Thomas Gomart, Kristijan Fidanovski, Laura K. Field, Jan-Werner Müller, Sergey Radchenko, Sophia Rosenfeld, Moritz Schularick, and Anna Wojciuk. The fellows pursue individual research topics, meet as a cohort several times throughout the year and contribute to public and policy debates through publications, events and media engagement.
Meet the 2026/2027 Cohort
Laura K. Field is one of the key analysts of the intellectual background to the American New Right, a subject of direct relevance for understanding the broader global illiberal turn. Her book Furious Minds: The Making of the MAGA New Right (Princeton University Press, 2025) provides the first rigorous insider-account of the network of academics, public intellectuals, and influencers (Patrick Deneen, Christopher Rufo, Peter Thiel, JD Vance and others) who have provided ideological fuel to the Trumpist revolution.
Thomas Gomart is the director of IFRI, France’s leading foreign policy think tank and one of Europe’s most influential, since 2015, and a historian of international relations by training, who combines scholarly depth with direct exposure to research and making of policy. His recent books (L’Affolement du monde (2019), Guerres invisibles (2021), Les Ambitions inavaouées (2023), and L’Accélération de l’histoire (2024)) constitute a sustained analytical account of a world veering beyond the frameworks of the international (liberal) order.
Sergey Radchenko is among the foremost historians of the Cold War and Soviet foreign policy, uniquely positioned to offer analysis of Russia’s contemporary behavior and the structural dynamics of great-power rivalry. His history “To Run the World: The Kremlin’s Cold War Bid for Global Power” (Cambridge University Press, 2024) making the point that Kremlin foreign policy has always been driven as much by the desire for recognition and legitimacy as by material interest is essential for understanding the origins of Russia’s war in Ukraine and its broader geopolitical context.
